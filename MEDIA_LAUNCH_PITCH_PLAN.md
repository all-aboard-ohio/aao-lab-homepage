# Media Launch and Pitch Plan

## Objective
Grow awareness, contributors, maintainers, and partner organizations for the project ecosystem quickly while positioning the work as a national open-source transit movement, not a single-state campaign.

## Core Positioning (Use in Every Pitch)
- This is an open-source transit civic-tech lab that any region can reuse.
- Ohio is the launchpad, not the limit.
- The repos are public, contribution-ready, and built for students, engineers, planners, journalists, and advocates.
- The mission is practical: better public understanding, better transit tooling, faster local action.

## Primary Outcomes (First 90 Days)
- 10x increase in weekly site visits.
- 5x increase in GitHub stars and watchers across repos.
- 100+ qualified new contributors in the funnel.
- 12-20 new recurring maintainers onboarded across projects.
- 20 media mentions (earned + creator content).
- 10 university or civic group partnerships outside Ohio.

## Audience Segments and Messaging

### 1) Transit Influencers (X, LinkedIn, Threads, Instagram)
What they care about:
- Policy impact, service quality, rider outcomes, advocacy signals.

Pitch angle:
- "Help your audience track and shape transit outcomes with open tools they can actually use."

CTA:
- Share one project.
- Quote-tweet a launch thread.
- Join one live demo stream.

### 2) Transit and Urbanism YouTubers
What they care about:
- Strong visuals, clear narrative arcs, practical examples, regional comparisons.

Pitch angle:
- "Feature a replicable, open-source transit toolkit your viewers can fork for their own city."

CTA:
- 8-12 minute feature.
- 1 interview segment with maintainer(s).
- Include contributor callout and links.

### 3) General Media (Local + National + Tech + Civic)
What they care about:
- Fresh civic angle, measurable public benefit, human stories, national relevance.

Pitch angle:
- "Volunteer engineers are building open infrastructure for transit literacy and accountability across regions."

CTA:
- Short interview.
- Coverage of launch milestone.
- Link to site and top repos.

## Message Architecture
Use this structure in every outreach email, DM, and press response:
1. Hook: "Open-source transit tools that communities can deploy anywhere."
2. Proof: "Live site, active repos, docs, and contributor pathways are already public."
3. Stakes: "Transit decisions happen fast; public understanding is lagging."
4. Invitation: "Cover it, feature it, or collaborate to localize it for your region."

## Offer Stack (What You Offer Creators and Media)
- Early access to roadmap previews.
- Exclusive data walkthroughs and demo recordings.
- Co-branded "Transit Tools for Your Region" livestream.
- State or metro-specific quickstart examples for their audience.
- Contributor challenge for their community (with leaderboard recognition).

## Launch Phases

## Phase 0: Prep (Days 1-7)
- Build a media kit folder:
  - 1-page project summary.
  - Logos, screenshots, short demo GIFs.
  - Founding story and maintainer bios.
  - Repo list with one-line value props.
- Publish "Start Here" page for new contributors.
- Add issue labels: good-first-issue, help-wanted, maintainer-track, content-needed.
- Prepare a national framing explainer: "How any state/city can fork this stack."

Exit criteria:
- Outreach assets complete.
- At least 20 clean onboarding issues tagged.
- All links tested.

## Phase 1: Quiet Seeding (Days 8-21)
- Hand-pick 40 creators/journalists and send personalized outreach.
- Run 5 private demos for friendly creators.
- Post a short launch teaser thread focused on "beyond Ohio" replication.
- Collect objections/questions and refine pitch copy.

Exit criteria:
- 8+ positive replies.
- 3+ committed coverage pieces.
- Baseline metrics recorded.

## Phase 2: Public Launch Sprint (Days 22-45)
- Publish coordinated launch assets:
  - Founder post on LinkedIn.
  - Thread on X.
  - Short YouTube explainer clip.
  - Press release style post in repo and site.
- Run a 7-day creator wave:
  - 2-3 influencer posts/day.
  - 1 long-form YouTube feature every 2-3 days.
- Host 2 live demos:
  - "How to contribute in 20 minutes."
  - "How to adapt this for your state/city."

Exit criteria:
- 100+ new contributors in top-of-funnel.
- 10+ pieces of creator/media coverage.
- Maintainer candidate list started.

## Phase 3: Conversion to Maintainers (Days 46-90)
- Launch a Maintainer Accelerator:
  - Weekly office hours.
  - Fast-track docs.
  - Mentored first triage/review tasks.
- Open 3 recurring cohorts:
  - Student contributors.
  - Professional engineers.
  - Transit-policy data volunteers.
- Publish monthly "State Replication Digest" with highlights from outside Ohio.

Exit criteria:
- 12-20 recurring maintainers.
- 10+ non-Ohio partner communities engaged.
- Stable monthly contributor growth.

## Conversion Funnel (Audience to Maintainer)
1. Awareness: creator/video/media mention.
2. Visit: site landing and project gallery.
3. Action: GitHub star/watch, newsletter, Slack/Discord join.
4. First contribution: docs fix, issue triage, small PR.
5. Ownership: recurring triage/review work.
6. Maintainer: merged governance and release responsibility.

## Rapid Growth Mechanics
- Every media hit must contain a direct contributor CTA.
- Every creator collaboration must include a "fork for your region" segment.
- Every week publish one traction metric snapshot to build momentum.
- Use social proof aggressively: logos, quote cards, contributor wins.
- Convert one-off contributors with 14-day follow-up nudges.

## Keeping Token Costs Low While Scaling
- Reuse modular prompt templates for outreach drafts.
- Batch-generate first-pass personalization, then human edit top 20 percent.
- Maintain a single source-of-truth pitch library to avoid re-prompting.
- Automate reporting with lightweight scripts and scheduled exports.
- Prioritize high-leverage channels with best conversion-to-maintainer ratios.

## KPI Dashboard
Track weekly:
- Site sessions and referral sources.
- Repo stars, watchers, forks, and first-time contributors.
- Open-to-merged PR cycle time.
- Number of active reviewers/maintainers.
- Outreach sent, response rate, and coverage secured.
- Share of non-Ohio participants and partner organizations.

## Risks and Mitigations
Risk: Project perceived as Ohio-only.
Mitigation:
- Lead with national replication examples and language in all assets.

Risk: Attention spikes but contributor retention is low.
Mitigation:
- Tight onboarding, office hours, and maintainer-track issues.

Risk: Media interest without technical conversion.
Mitigation:
- Include direct repo/task CTA in every interview and article.

Risk: Maintainer burnout.
Mitigation:
- Rotate ownership, enforce lightweight governance, and publish clear escalation paths.
